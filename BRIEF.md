2. Clicker as trigger — not a draw-length exam. Subconscious execution. Set the clicker to the draw length, not the other way around. Movement before, during, after the click.
3. Kinetic chain — force from the ground up. Inside edge of the rear foot into the bow arm.
4. Structure — shoulders over heels. “Pringles tube.” Head up, tongue to the roof, rear shoulder down.
5. How you learn — focus / failure / cerebellum pose, then 5–10 min quiet after the session.
6. Wind — 3D aiming does not do everything. Predict the environment.

## /3d-aiming

The clicker is in the middle of the movement.

You are not aiming on a flat picture. You are sending force down a line — up/down, left/right, and into the target. Longer direction, more control. The tube to the ten. Make the pipe a funnel.

How it works:
- Keep moving after the click. The shot finishes after the arrow has left.
- Push the bow. Aim with the wrist-watch. Front hand snaps. Second position.
- Horsepower from the shoulder and chest. Intelligence from the hand.
- Connect to the ground. Do not shoot a light, neutrally balanced bow. A 3–4" metal extender helps the force go forward.
- Outdoors, pick your spot.

How you learn it:
- Shooting-band drill. Over-exaggerate. Fake it until the dots join, even with a delay.
- Blank boss helps. Relax and let it happen.

If you take one thing: direction to the target is the skill. Conventional “keep the arm up” is not enough.

## /clicker

The clicker is not a test you sit when you are ready. It is how the shot becomes subconscious.

Its obvious job is draw length. Its real job is a trigger. Without it, people get stiff, they decide when to shoot, and they wait years for “consistency” that never arrives. Every top recurve uses one. Learn that habit now.

Set the clicker to your draw length. Do not drag your draw length to the clicker.

Starting gaps: beginner 12–18mm, intermediate 7–15mm, advanced 4–8mm, elite 1.5–5mm. Set a little short of full draw and move through. Execute on the click. Movement before, during, and after.

“Pulling through the clicker” is only half. Push forwards. Accept the float. One pressure. Don’t make tension. Make movement.

Stuck under it: move. Both sides. Lead with the elbow, never the hand first. Long, relaxed inside-arm lines.

A sight clicker on the extension bar is excellent for new archers. Close target, or a big face, first time.

## /front-arm

Club coaches say “keep the arm up.” They do not tell you how.

Usually the force is pointing down: shoulder higher than the hand, bow-side ribs high, spine bowed away. The arm has nowhere to go but drop.

Fix the structure, then the intent:
- A 3–4" metal extender. Forward momentum. Do not balance the bow to neutral.
- 3D Aiming: change the intent. Force into the target. Snap the wrist. Aim with the watch.
- Shoulder: inside-out. Lats on the draw. Pecs like pegging one side of a tent.
- Ribcage: base of the ribs away from the target. Mid-spine, not a shrug.
- Train it: side planks with control, shooting-band, keep the line after the shot.

The arm stays up when the skeleton and the force agree.
